Understanding Rates

Why rates differ

Buy vs sell explained

The buy rate is what you receive when you sell foreign currency to us. The sell rate is what you pay when you buy foreign currency. The gap between them covers handling and processing costs.

Interbank vs retail

Interbank rates are wholesale prices between banks. Retail rates include a margin that covers operational costs, compliance, and cash-handling — which is why branch rates differ slightly from live feeds.

When to lock

Locking a rate protects you from unfavourable movement before your travel date or transfer. If you expect the rupee to weaken, locking early can save you money compared to a walk-in rate.

The spread is the difference between the buy and sell rate. It covers the dealer's margin, liquidity costs, and transaction handling. Major currencies like USD and EUR typically have tighter spreads, while less-traded currencies may have wider spreads to account for lower liquidity.
